> Rule (CLAUDE.md §7.3): every deliberate SEO change gets an entry BEFORE its
> result is known — date, change, hypothesis, result. Results are evaluated

## Evaluation pass — 2026-09-11

Thirteen entries below carried a `pending` ranking or usage verdict whose stated
window (2–4 weeks for impressions, 2–4 months for positions) had expired. They
are resolved here against the first real Search Console capture of the project,
[`baseline/2026-09-11/search-console.md`](baseline/2026-09-11/search-console.md)
— GSC domain property, search type Web, window 2026-07-11 → 2026-09-09.

Three limits bind every reading below. They are stated once here instead of
being repeated in each row:

1. **GSC average position is across all queries a URL was served for, not its
   `primaryQuery`.** None of the declared primary queries appears in the named
   query list: 80 % of the site's impressions sit below GSC's anonymity
   threshold. So a page's position number describes the page, **not** the
   hypothesis's target query. Where a row says a position is inside the 1–20
   band, that is a property of the URL, not a claim that the primary query
   ranks there.
2. **The site has been frozen since 2026-07-26.** No deploy, no new content, no
   distribution. Everything below is Google's response to a static corpus, which
   makes the readings cleaner than usual and makes every O5 result a measurement
   of *distribution*, not of the artefact.
3. **n is tiny.** 4 clicks and 1 469 impressions in three months. No percentage
   derived from single-digit clicks is carried forward as a rate.

The window for objective **O2** (positions 1–20) is 2–4 months and this pass is
at month 2. Rows are therefore marked *provisional* where the page is already
inside the band: the verdict can still move in either direction.

## Pilot closure and v2 pre-registration — 2026-09-20

### Why this round is a pilot, and why saying so is not a reclassification

H1–H6 were registered on 2026-07-25 as confirmatory hypotheses with 90-day
windows closing ≈2026-10-23. They are being closed early, at the redesign
deploy, and the round is being reported as a **pilot**. The distinction that
keeps this honest:

- The numbers are published **as observed**, with the real window and the
  bounds recalculated for it — not withdrawn, not relabelled away. Publication
  rule 6 still binds: a falsified hypothesis is still reported.
- The *reason* this was a pilot in fact, and not in hindsight, is already on the
  record above: three collection defects were found while the instrument was
  running (Defect C, RSC prefetches contaminating `ua_class = unknown`; Defect
  A, `bot_signer` never reaching GA4; Defect B, the draft-05 Dictionary form of
  `Signature-Agent` rejected), the Axis A `high` tier was unreachable as first
  written, and H5 was voided once and re-opened. A round that spends its window
  discovering that its instrument is wrong is a pilot, whatever it was called
  when it started.
- What is being claimed is therefore narrow: **this round produced a working
  instrument and a list of design defects, not effect sizes.**

**The cost of stopping early, now computed rather than estimated.** It is
smaller than the calendar suggests, because the bound that matters is stated
against **exposure**, not against time. Trap A's zero is bounded by the 1,191
fetches of `/robots.txt` that actually happened, not by the 57 days they
happened in — and 1,191 reads give a 95% upper bound of 0.25%, which a further
33 days would have moved very little. Where the pilot is genuinely weak is the
channel nobody reads: nine real fetches of `/llms.txt` bound Trap B only at
28%, and no realistic extension of the window fixes that, because the problem
is exposure, not duration. H5 is the clearest case — see its verdict below.
Publish the interval, never the word "zero".

### The lessons — the pilot's actual deliverable

1. **A detection instrument fails silently.** None of the three defects raised
   an error anywhere. Two discarded valid data and one contaminated an entire
   bucket. All three were found only by reading exports by hand.
2. **A zero without a positive control is not a measurement.** With no channel
   on a surface known to be consumed, "nothing came" and "the instrument is
   broken" are the same observation. This is the pilot's central design defect
   and the thing v2 fixes first.
3. **A zero without a denominator is not a result.** "Of N reads of
   `/llms.txt`, zero followed a link in it" is a finding; "0 hits" is not. The
   pilot already has the denominators it needs for two channels.
4. **The traffic that matters does not identify itself.** No agent documented
   as training-purpose fetched a disallowed page. The volume sits in the
   unidentified bucket, which is what H3 was for.
5. **The vendor moves underneath the method.** Anthropic and Common Crawl left
   the `unverifiable` class mid-window, which forced the analysis to be split at
   a cutoff. Signals in this field have a half-life measured in months — now
   publication rule 9.
6. **Publishing the method publishes the bait.** The trap slugs live in tracked
   files in a public repository, which `github.com` and code search expose to
   the same agents the experiment measures (§4.5). Git history is permanent, so
   traps A and B carry that confound for good and Trap B's channel-adoption
   claim does not survive it unqualified. v2 answers with both halves: new
   channel slugs are born in environment variables and never enter a tracked
   file, and a repository-only canary measures the size of the hole. The general
   version — reproducible detection work makes its own bait discoverable — looks
   unmeasured in the literature.
7. **Pre-registering a hypothesis without pre-registering its power produces a
   test that could not have worked.** H5 required at least five fetches of one
   article in fourteen days. This domain is crawled at roughly half a fetch per
   article per fortnight — an order of magnitude short, knowable in advance from
   the crawl volume already in the property. The fix is a line in the
   registration: what is the smallest effect this window can detect, and does
   the observed traffic reach it?
8. **A rotating pseudonymous identifier makes self-exclusion a per-period
   operation, and a VPN multiplies it again.** One physical connection produced
   three `net_id` values across the pilot, of which the first analysis excluded
   one; a VPN exit produced a fourth. Owner traffic turned out to be 24% of the
   raw dataset. The identifier design is right — it is supposed to stop
   accumulating — but an exclusion list written as "my network" rather than "my
   network, per month, per exit" is incomplete by construction, and silently so.
