This site measured every request it received for 57 days, from 2026-07-25 to
2026-09-20, to answer a narrow question: **which automated clients read it,
whether they are who they claim to be, and whether they follow its
robots.txt**. The pilot confirmed two of its six pre-registered hypotheses,
falsified two, and found six defects in its own instrument, all of them
silent. A positive control in September then showed that the instrument sees
every AI assistant visit it should. What follows is the method, the numbers,
and what went wrong. Every figure traces back to the public [experiment
log](https://github.com/Alf4kill/seotecnico.dev.br/blob/main/docs/experiment-log.md).

## The instrument

The site runs on Next.js on Vercel. Its proxy (Next.js middleware) records
every document request and every fetch of `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml`,
`llms.txt` and the feed as a server-side event in a separate analytics
property. Static assets and the site's own client navigation are excluded.
Each event carries:

- **The claimed agent**, read from the user agent. A claim, not an identity.
- **A verification verdict**, computed only for clients that claim a known
  agent or sign the request. The verdict is one of `verified-ip` (inside the
  vendor’s published range), `verified-rdns` (forward-confirmed reverse DNS),
  `verified-signature` (a valid Web Bot Auth signature), `impersonated`
  (claims a vendor that publishes sources, and fails them) or
  `unverifiable` (the vendor publishes nothing to check against). A missing
  source never becomes an accusation.
- **Request-shape signals**, such as whether the browser-only `Sec-Fetch-*`
  headers are present and whether the request is conditional.
- **A network identifier** for correlation without identification: a salted,
  truncated hash of the /24 (IPv4) or /48 (IPv6) prefix, with a salt that
  rotates every month. The IP address itself never leaves the server.

Two honeypot URLs, each designed to be reachable through exactly one channel,
measured policy behaviour. Trap A existed only as a `Disallow:` line in robots.txt;
fetching it means the client read the file and fetched what it forbids. Trap
B existed only as a link in `llms.txt`. Six hypotheses, with windows and
falsification conditions, were written into the log before any data existed.

## What the pilot found

The window closed at 57 of the planned 90 days, when a site-wide redesign
shipped. The early close was declared, not hidden, and the round is reported
as a pilot. It recorded **7,208 events, 5,480 after excluding the owner’s own
traffic**, which turned out to be 24% of everything recorded.

| # | Hypothesis | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | A client reads robots.txt and fetches the trap it forbids | **Falsified** | Zero real trap hits against 1,191 robots.txt fetches: following the `Disallow` line happens in at most 0.25% of reads (one-sided 95%) |
| H2 | A verified training crawler fetches a disallowed page | **Confirmed** | 2026-07-30: GPTBot, `verified-ip`, three disallowed paths |
| H3 | Undeclared automation outnumbers declared-and-disallowed agents | **Confirmed** | 436 networks vs 7, **62×** by networks, 3.1× by events |
| H4 | Conditional requests reveal retrieval-purpose agents | **Falsified** | Claude-SearchBot 0/939, OAI-SearchBot 0/182; Bingbot 52/96; 93.6% of conditional requests came from undeclared clients |
| H5 | Refreshing an article’s `dateModified` draws more retrieval fetches | **Inconclusive** | Underpowered by about 10×: the test needed 5 fetches of one article in 14 days, and the site gets 0.47 |
| H6 | Nothing follows links in `llms.txt` | **Sustained, uselessly** | Zero trap B hits, but only 9 reads of `llms.txt` in 57 days: the bound is ≤ 28% |

Three of these need their qualifiers attached.

**H2 is not "GPTBot ignored robots.txt."** OpenAI documents GPTBot as a
training crawler, and this site’s robots.txt had disallowed it since
2026-07-25. The three requests on 2026-07-30 came from inside OpenAI’s
published range. But GPTBot never fetched robots.txt during the window. What
the data supports is "fetched disallowed paths without having read the
directive here", and the difference between that and "ignored the rule" is
the difference between a measurement and an accusation. Three requests on
one date prove the behaviour exists. They do not give a rate.

**H3 is a lower bound in one direction and an upper estimate in another.**
It was computed on requests without `Sec-Fetch-*` headers, a conservative
proxy for "not a browser". But a matcher defect found later (below) counted
share-image fetches as page reads, and link-preview bots rarely declare
themselves. The direction is safe at 62×; the exact size is not.

**H1 and H6 carry a confound that cannot be removed.** Both trap slugs were
in tracked files of this site’s public repository, which the same agents
crawl. A trap hit could have come from reading the repository. For Trap A the
claim survives, because a `Disallow` line applies however the URL was found.
For Trap B, any hit would not have proven that a client follows `llms.txt`.

## Six defects, all silent

None of these raised an error. Each one was found by reading exports by hand
or by checking an odd number.

1. **The signer was never recorded.** A valid Web Bot Auth signature proves
   only that the request was signed by a key published at the domain named in
   `Signature-Agent`. That domain is the identity, and it never reached the
   analytics property: 64 `verified-signature` events have no signer.
2. **The current signature format was rejected.** A 2026 draft of the Web Bot
   Auth architecture changed `Signature-Agent` to a structured Dictionary. The
   verifier accepted only the older form.
3. **Human browsing was counted as automation.** Next.js prefetches visible
   links with requests that do not ask for HTML, and those passed the matcher
   as "unknown" clients. Of the owner’s own provider-network events, 994 of
   1,041 were prefetches.
4. **The public repository is itself a discovery channel.** Publishing the
   method published the bait. Git history is permanent, so the confound on the
   two traps can only be bounded, never removed.
5. **One connection produced several network identifiers.** Because the salt
   rotates monthly, the owner’s single connection appeared under three
   identifiers across the window, and a VPN exit under a fourth. The first
   analysis excluded only one of them.
6. **Share images counted as page reads.** The matcher excluded
   `opengraph-image` only at the start of a path, so every nested card, such
   as `/blog/<slug>/opengraph-image`, was recorded as a document fetch. It was
   found while closing the positive control and fixed on 2026-09-25.

The lesson that generalises best: **pre-registering a hypothesis without
pre-registering its power produces a test that could not have worked.** H5
was knowable in advance to be ten times short, from the crawl volume the
property already held. Every hypothesis on this site now states the smallest
effect its window can detect before it is registered.

## The positive control: does the instrument see a known visit?

With zero hits and no control, "nothing came" and "the instrument is broken"
are the same observation. So in September the site ran one on purpose: 15
dated prompts, five each to ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity, asking each to
open a page and report the codes on it.

The page lived at a secret URL stored only in an environment variable, since
the assistants under test can search GitHub. It carried four codes, one per
rendering path: text from a Server Component, text rendered by a
`'use client'` component, a value that existed only in the JSON-LD, and a
value fetched in a `useEffect` after mount. Every code was derived from an
HMAC of the secret and the round number, so each round had new codes.

| Assistant | Opened the page | Server text | `'use client'` text | JSON-LD only | After JavaScript | Requests seen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT | 5 of 5 | 5 of 5 | 5 of 5 | 0 of 5 | 0 of 5 | 5, `verified-ip` |
| Claude | 5 of 5 | 5 of 5 | 5 of 5 | 0 of 5 | 0 of 5 | 5, `verified-ip` |
| Perplexity (free plan) | 0 of 5 | — | — | — | — | 0 |

**The instrument saw every visit.** Every correct answer needed a request to
this server, because the codes existed nowhere else. The proxy recorded five
verified requests per vendor, one per round. No assistant invented a code:
60 were asked for, and every one that did not reach an assistant was
reported as not found.

**What the assistants read** is the text of the server-rendered HTML. Output
from a `'use client'` component arrived every time, because client
components are rendered on the server too. Neither the JSON-LD nor anything
that required JavaScript ever arrived. Claude described its own tool: fetch
the HTML, extract the text, drop `<script>` blocks. The full write-up is in
Portuguese: [what ChatGPT reads from a Next.js
page](/blog/chatgpt-le-javascript).

**Perplexity, on the free plan, answered from its index.** Its user-triggered
agent made zero requests to this domain in two months, against six verified
fetches by its indexer. Asked about the home page, which the indexer had
crawled, it answered correctly with no request reaching the server. Asked
open-endedly about an article the indexer had never visited, it wrote a
confident analysis inferred from the words in the URL, with the admission
that it had not loaded the page buried in the middle.

**One more near-miss for the method.** An analytics export taken a few hours
after the rounds showed 3 and 2 requests instead of 5 and 5. The property had
not finished processing the day. Read at face value, it would have reported
five instrument failures that never happened. Exports are now analysed only
after the day has been closed for 24 hours, and the 5 and 5 figure is being
re-confirmed under that rule.

## What is measured now

- **v2 of the detection instrument** (H7–H13, pre-registered before its code):
  a coherence vector over request headers, new discovery channels whose slugs
  never enter a tracked file, and a repository-only canary that measures how
  often the public repository itself is crawled for URLs.
- **H14, from 2026-09-25 to 2026-12-24:** does anything ask this site for
  Markdown with `Accept: text/markdown`? Measurement only; every response stays
  HTML. Serving Markdown is built only if at least five distinct networks or
  two verified agents ask for it.
